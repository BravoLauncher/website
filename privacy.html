Privacy Policy

Bravo Launcher (com.ergton.bravolauncher) · Developer: Ergton · Effective date: 17 September 2026

In one sentence

Bravo Launcher does not collect, transmit, share or sell any data — it has no servers, no analytics, no ads, and everything it stores stays on your device.

1Who we are and what this covers

This policy describes how Bravo Launcher ("the app"), published by Ergton ("we"), handles information. It applies to the Android application distributed on Google Play under the package name com.ergton.bravolauncher.

2Data we collect: none

The app does not collect, transmit, or share any personal data or usage data. Specifically, the app contains:

  • No analytics or tracking of any kind (no Firebase Analytics, no advertising identifiers, no telemetry).
  • No advertising SDKs.
  • No crash-reporting services.
  • No network communication. The app makes no connections to the internet and operates entirely offline. We operate no servers, and there are no accounts or sign-ins.

3Data stored on your device only

To do its job as a home screen, the app stores the following locally on your device. This information never leaves your device through the app:

  • Your home screen, dock and folder layout (which apps and widgets are placed where).
  • How often and when you launch apps from the launcher — used only to offer "frequent" and "recent" sorting in the app drawer.
  • Your settings and preferences (grid size, theme, gestures, and similar).
  • Custom app names and icons you choose, and a local cache of app icons.
  • Your lists of hidden and locked apps.
  • Backup files the app writes automatically after you change things (see section 6), in its own storage or in a folder you picked for them.

Uninstalling the app permanently deletes all of this data.

4Permissions, explained honestly

Android requires apps to declare permissions for certain features. Here is every notable permission Bravo Launcher uses and why. Every sensitive permission is optional: they are grouped in the app under Settings → Permissions, where each one shows what it is used for and can be granted or revoked at any time. The launcher works without them — a feature that needs a missing permission simply shows a warning instead of failing silently.

PermissionWhy the app has it
Query installed appsCore launcher function: showing your apps in the drawer and on the home screen. The list is never transmitted anywhere.
Notification accessOptional, for notification badges. The app counts notifications per app to show a dot or number. It never reads, stores or transmits notification content — titles, messages and senders are not accessed.
Accessibility serviceOptional, for back-tap and side-tap gestures while other apps are in the foreground. The service only reads the motion sensor (accelerometer) and draws the brief on-screen confirmation shown after a tap action (which you can turn off in Gesture settings). It is configured so it cannot read screen content and cannot perform gestures on your behalf.
BiometricsOptional, for the app-lock feature. Fingerprint/face verification is handled entirely by Android; the app never sees your biometric data.
Do Not Disturb accessOptional, only if you assign a gesture that toggles ring/vibrate mode.
Modify system settingsOptional, only if you assign a gesture that toggles auto-rotate. The app writes the system's screen-rotation setting and nothing else.
Set wallpaperLets you change your wallpaper from within the launcher.

VibrationHaptic feedback, including the optional confirmation buzz when a tap gesture fires.

5Web search and third-party search providers

The search bar can optionally hand a query to the web-search provider you choose in the app's settings. When you do, the app simply opens your own browser at that provider's website with your query — the app itself sends nothing and receives nothing.

The moment your query leaves the launcher, it is outside the scope of this policy and has nothing to do with us. How your data is handled from that point is entirely the responsibility of the search provider, under its own privacy policy and terms and conditions — please read them on the provider's website before using it. These providers are independent of Ergton; some may ask you to sign in to their own service in your browser, and that relationship is between you and them. If you never use web search, no query ever leaves your device.

6Backups

Automatic backups: shortly after you finish changing the launcher, it writes a backup file (your layout and settings, the same content as a manual backup). By default these files stay in the app's own private storage, where no other app can read them and where they are deleted with the app. You choose how many to keep (5 to 50) or turn the feature off in Settings → Backup & Restore, and you can delete any backup from the list there. The launcher never uploads these files anywhere.

Manual backup and the backup folder: the first manual backup asks you to pick a folder, which the app uses only for its own backups; from then on both automatic and manual backups are written there, and the app keeps that folder's access permission only for that purpose. If the folder you pick is watched by a sync or cloud app of yours, that app copies the files under its own terms, not ours — the choice is entirely yours, and nothing is synced unless you set that up. You can change the folder at any time; existing backups are moved along.

Restoring: restoring replaces your current layout and settings with the backup's and restarts the launcher. It reads only the file you chose from the list or picked yourself.

Android system backup: like most apps, Bravo Launcher allows Android's built-in backup to include its settings in your device backup (for example to your own Google account, if you have device backup enabled). That process is operated by Google under Google's terms, not by us, and you can disable it in your device's backup settings. The launcher's own backup files described above are excluded from it, so they are never copied to Google by the app's configuration.

Theme files: exporting a theme writes a plain-text file containing your appearance settings and, if you include the layout, the package names of the apps placed on your home screen and dock, your folder names, and the identifiers of your widgets. It never contains your accounts, widget contents, custom icons, hidden apps, gestures or anything from inside your apps. Bear in mind that sharing a theme file shares that list of apps with whoever receives it. Importing a theme only ever reads a file you pick yourself; the app does not download themes from anywhere, and a theme file cannot run code, change gestures or reveal hidden apps.

7Children

The app is not directed at children. Since the app collects no data from anyone, it collects no data from children.

8Changes to this policy

If the app's behavior ever changes in a way that affects privacy, we will update this page and its effective date before releasing that change. The current version always lives at this address.

9Contact

Bug reports and debug information (tap trace exports, crash logs): please open an issue on github.com/BravoLauncher/BravoLauncher and attach the exported files there. Questions about this policy, and security reports: bravolauncher@ergton.com.
